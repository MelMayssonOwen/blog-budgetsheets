The best free budget spreadsheet templates in 2026

Updated August 2026

The best genuinely-free budget spreadsheet templates in 2026 are Vertex42’s Money Management Template (the proven incumbent — “Over 750,000 downloads” on its own page), the built-in budget templates in Google Sheets (best for sharing and mobile), Microsoft Excel’s budget templates (best if you already own Office), and the CFPB Budget Worksheet (best neutral, government-published starting point). All four are free and manual. Below, what each is best at and the catch in each — then when paying starts to make sense.

1. Vertex42 Money Management Template — the proven incumbent

Two decades of use and download counts to match: over 750,000 for the original and over 250,000 for v2.1 on Vertex42’s own page. Free under a personal-use license. Best for people who want a complete, battle-tested manual budget and don’t mind a busy layout.

2. Google Sheets budget template — best for sharing

Free, cloud-native, and easy to share with a partner in real time, with a mobile app for on-the-go entry. The starter template is basic, so power users graduate to a custom or purchased sheet — but for a first budget it’s ideal.

3. Microsoft Excel templates — best if you own Office

Excel ships free budget templates. If you already pay for Microsoft 365, there’s no reason to look elsewhere for a manual sheet.

4. CFPB Budget Worksheet — the neutral official option

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau publishes a free budget worksheet plus a spending tracker and bill calendar. Government-published, no upsell — a clean, trustworthy starting grid.

When to stop using free

Move to paid only for automation (Tiller, $99/yr, adds bank sync) or design (a one-time template if a nicer layout would keep you consistent). Free covers the budgeting itself completely.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free budget spreadsheet?
Vertex42's Money Management Template is the most proven free option, with over 750,000 downloads on its own page and a free personal-use license. For sharing with a partner, the free Google Sheets budget template is better; for a neutral official starter, the CFPB Budget Worksheet. All are free and manual.
Is Google Sheets or Excel better for a free budget?
Google Sheets is better for sharing with a partner and mobile entry, and it's free with a Google account. Excel's templates are also free but Excel itself needs a paid Microsoft 365 license. If you already own Office, use Excel; otherwise Google Sheets is the free default.
Are free budget templates good enough, or should I pay?
Free templates fully cover manual budgeting — you only pay for convenience. Pay about $99/year for Tiller if you want automatic bank sync, or a one-time $9–$29 for a better-designed template. Neither adds budgeting capability free templates lack.
Does the CFPB have a free budget template?
Yes. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau publishes a free Budget Worksheet along with a Spending Tracker and Bill Calendar. They're government-published with no upsell, making them a trustworthy, neutral starting point.
