The best budget spreadsheets for couples in 2026

Updated August 2026

The best budget spreadsheet for a couple is one both partners can open and edit at the same time, so a cloud spreadsheet you both share beats a downloaded file. The strongest free option is a Google Sheets budget (real-time shared editing, mobile app for both people, $0). If you want automation across joint accounts, Tiller ($99/year) syncs shared bank and card transactions into one sheet. If you want a ready-made joint layout without a subscription, one-time couples budget templates ($15, $25 range) exist. The deciding features for two people are shared editing, a clear split of who-pays-what, and both partners seeing the same numbers.

What matters for couplesPick for shared real-time editing first (Google Sheets or a cloud-hosted template), then decide on automation (Tiller for joint-account sync) vs manual (free or one-time template). Both people needing the same live numbers is the whole game.

1. Google Sheets, the free shared default

Two people, one live document, real-time edits, free. Add a “who paid” column and a monthly split calculation and you have a complete joint budget. Best starting point for most couples.

2. Tiller, when you want joint accounts to sync themselves

If you share bank and card accounts, Tiller ($99/yr) pulls both partners’ transactions into one Google Sheet automatically, so neither person has to enter them. Worth it when manual entry is what makes a shared budget fall apart.

3. A one-time couples template, polish without a subscription

If you want a joint layout that’s ready on day one, shared expenses, individual columns, a split calculation, a one-time-purchase couples template gives you that for a single payment, still manual.

Worth a lookOne example of a ready-made joint layout is sheetfolk’s Couples Budget Spreadsheet, its “most popular” template at $17 (one-time, lifetime ownership, works in Google Sheets so both partners can edit); sheetfolk also has a free manual template if you want to try the format before paying. Frequently asked questions

What's the best budget spreadsheet for couples?
A shared cloud spreadsheet both partners can edit live. The best free option is a Google Sheets budget ($0, real-time editing, mobile apps for both). If you want joint accounts to sync automatically, Tiller ($99/year) is best; if you want a ready-made joint layout without a subscription, a one-time couples template works.
How do couples split expenses in a spreadsheet?
Add a 'who paid' column and a running total per person, then a formula that calculates what each partner owes the other to reach your agreed split (50/50, proportional to income, or whatever you decide). Any spreadsheet, free or paid, can do this; it's just arithmetic.
Should couples use one budget or two?
That's a personal choice, but a shared spreadsheet works for both: one combined sheet for joint expenses, or separate tabs for individual spending plus a shared tab for common bills. The key is that both partners can see the same numbers, which favors a cloud spreadsheet over a downloaded file.
Is a paid couples template worth it over free Google Sheets?
Only for the ready-made layout. A one-time couples template (typically $15, $25) gives you shared-expense columns, individual tracking, and a split calculation already built. Free Google Sheets does the same once you set it up yourself. Buy the template to save setup time, not for extra capability.
