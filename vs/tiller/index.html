A spreadsheet vs Tiller: do you need $99/year bank sync?

Updated August 2026

Use a plain spreadsheet if you are willing to type your transactions and want to spend $0; pay for Tiller if manual entry is the specific thing that has made you quit budgeting before. Tiller costs $99/year (30-day free trial, per Tiller’s pricing page) and its one real advantage is automation: it imports your bank and card transactions into a Google Sheet or Excel file and auto-categorizes them with AutoCat. A free or one-time-purchase template does everything else a budget needs, you just enter the numbers yourself.

Decision in one lineThe only thing Tiller sells that a free spreadsheet can’t give you is automatic transaction import. If that’s your blocker, $99/yr is cheap. If it isn’t, you’re paying a subscription for math a free sheet already does.

When Tiller earns its price

Tiller is worth it when the friction of manual entry is what breaks your consistency, the CFPB is blunt that “making, and sticking, with a budget takes effort,” and removing the highest-friction step is a legitimate way to keep going. If you have many accounts and cards, automation compounds.

When a spreadsheet wins

A free or one-time template wins when you want zero recurring cost, full control over the layout, and don’t connect a bank at all (some people won’t link accounts to a third party). You still get every method, 50/30/20, zero-based, plan-vs-actual, because those are just formulas.

Frequently asked questions

Is Tiller better than a free spreadsheet?
Only for automation. Tiller's $99/year buys automatic bank-transaction import and categorization into your own Google Sheet or Excel file. Everything else a budget needs, a free spreadsheet already does. If manual data entry is your blocker, Tiller is better; if not, a free sheet does the same job for $0.
Can I cancel Tiller and keep my spreadsheet?
Yes. Tiller's pricing page says you can cancel anytime and the spreadsheet is yours, but the automatic transaction feed stops when the subscription ends, so from then on you'd enter transactions manually.
Does Tiller have a free trial?
Yes, 30 days per Tiller's pricing page, and your card isn't charged until the trial ends. That's enough to test whether automated sync actually keeps you consistent before you pay.
