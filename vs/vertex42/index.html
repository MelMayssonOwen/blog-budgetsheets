Paid budget templates vs Vertex42 (free): is it ever worth paying?

Updated August 2026

Vertex42’s budget templates are free and well-proven, the Money Management Template shows “Over 750,000 downloads” on Vertex42’s own page. You should pay for an alternative only when you want something Vertex42 doesn’t give: automatic bank sync (that’s Tiller, $99/year) or a more modern, ready-to-use layout with a dashboard and protected cells (a one-time-purchase template, typically $9, $29). For plain manual budgeting on any method, free Vertex42 is genuinely enough.

Bottom lineVertex42 is free and does the whole job manually. Pay a competitor only to buy automation (Tiller) or polish/layout (a one-time template), never because free is somehow incomplete. It isn’t.

Reasons to pay anyway

Two legitimate ones: (1) you want automatic transaction import, only Tiller does that among spreadsheet options; (2) the free layout is dense and you’d actually keep a budget with a cleaner, ready-made design. If neither applies, keep the money.

Frequently asked questions

Is Vertex42 really free?
Yes. Vertex42's Money Management Template and its core budgeting templates are free under a personal-use license, meaning you can use them but not resell or redistribute them. Vertex42 also sells some premium/Pro templates, but the main budget templates cost nothing.
Why would I pay when Vertex42 is free?
Two reasons only: to get automatic bank sync (Tiller, $99/year), which Vertex42 doesn't offer; or to get a more modern, ready-made layout with a dashboard if the free design would stop you from keeping a budget. For the underlying budgeting math, free is enough.
Is a $17 one-time template better than a free Vertex42 sheet?
Not functionally, both are manual. A paid one-time template can be better designed and faster to start with, which is a real convenience, but it doesn't do anything the free sheet can't. Buy it for the layout and time saved, not for capability.
