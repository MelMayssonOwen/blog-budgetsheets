Budget spreadsheet vs an app like YNAB or Monarch: which should you use?

Updated August 2026

Use a spreadsheet if you want $0 cost, full control of the layout, and don’t mind entering transactions; use an app if automatic bank sync and a phone-first experience are what keep you consistent. A manual spreadsheet is free. YNAB is $14.99/month or $109/year with a 34-day free trial (ynab.com/pricing) and enforces its own zero-based “give every dollar a job” method. Monarch is a paid dashboard app with a one-week trial. The apps buy you automation and structure; the spreadsheet buys you control and no recurring bill.

The honest tradeApps remove the two hardest parts of spreadsheet budgeting, data entry and a fixed method, but charge ~$100/year and lock you into their way of working. A spreadsheet gives you everything for $0 if you’ll do the typing and design the method yourself.

Choose a spreadsheet if…

You want zero recurring cost, you like tinkering with your own layout, you want to switch methods freely, or you’d rather not link bank accounts to a third-party app. A spreadsheet also travels with you forever, no subscription means no lockout.

Choose an app if…

Manual entry is what has made you abandon budgeting before, you want everything on your phone, or you specifically want YNAB’s zero-based discipline. If you want the spreadsheet and automation, Tiller ($99/yr) is the middle path.

Frequently asked questions

Is a spreadsheet or YNAB better for budgeting?
A spreadsheet is better if you want $0 cost, total control, and don't mind manual entry. YNAB is better if you want automatic bank sync, a phone-first app, and its enforced zero-based method to keep you disciplined. YNAB is $14.99/month or $109/year with a 34-day trial; a spreadsheet is free.
Why pay for YNAB when a spreadsheet is free?
You're paying for automation and structure: automatic transaction import, a polished app on every device, and a built-in method that stops you overspending a category. If you'd keep a spreadsheet consistently anyway, you don't need to pay. If manual entry makes you quit, the ~$109/year can be what keeps you budgeting.
Can a spreadsheet do zero-based budgeting like YNAB?
Yes. Zero-based budgeting, assigning every dollar a job until income minus allocations equals zero, is just a formula, and any spreadsheet can do it. YNAB automates and enforces it; a spreadsheet lets you do the same thing manually for free. See our zero-based budgeting guide.
Is it safe to link my bank to a budgeting app?
Reputable apps use read-only aggregation (they can see transactions, not move money) and you can revoke access. If you'd rather not link accounts at all, a manual spreadsheet avoids the question entirely. The CFPB publishes guidance on financial data sharing worth reading first.
