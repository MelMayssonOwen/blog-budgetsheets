Tiller alternatives in 2026: cheaper and free ways to budget in a spreadsheet

Updated August 2026

Tiller is $99/year and its headline feature is automatic bank sync into a Google Sheet or Excel file (tiller.com/pricing). If you want the spreadsheet workflow without the subscription, the main alternatives are: a free manual template (Vertex42, Google Sheets, Microsoft) if you’ll enter transactions yourself; a one-time-purchase template ($9, $29) for a nicer manual layout; or a budgeting app like YNAB ($109/yr) or Monarch if you’d rather leave spreadsheets entirely. Only Tiller keeps you inside a real spreadsheet and automates entry, the trade is subscription vs manual work.

Key trade-offEvery Tiller alternative drops one of Tiller’s two properties: it’s either a spreadsheet but manual (free/one-time templates) or automated but not a spreadsheet (YNAB, Monarch). Pick which property you can live without.

Go free-manual (Vertex42) or one-time template. You lose automatic import, so budget time to enter transactions, the CFPB’s free budget worksheet and spending tracker are a $0 starting point.

Frequently asked questions

What's the cheapest Tiller alternative?
A free manual template from Vertex42, Google Sheets or Microsoft, $0. You give up Tiller's automatic bank sync and enter transactions yourself. If you want a nicer layout without a subscription, a one-time-purchase template is $9, $29 once.
Is there a free version of Tiller?
No. Tiller is $99/year after a 30-day trial. The free equivalent is a manual spreadsheet template, same budgeting math, but you import and categorize transactions yourself instead of Tiller doing it automatically.
Is YNAB a good Tiller alternative?
It's an alternative to budgeting in a spreadsheet at all. YNAB ($14.99/month or $109/year) is an app with its own zero-based method and bank sync, not a spreadsheet. Choose it if you'd rather leave spreadsheets; stay with Tiller or a template if the spreadsheet workflow is the point.
