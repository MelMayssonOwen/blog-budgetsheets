Budget spreadsheet FAQ: free vs paid, methods, and apps

Updated August 2026

Short, direct answers to the questions people actually ask about budget spreadsheet templates in 2026, whether to pay, which method to use, and whether a spreadsheet or an app is right. Every number below is quoted from the named vendor’s own page and linked in the sources list.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to pay for a budget spreadsheet?
No. Vertex42, Google Sheets, Microsoft Excel and the CFPB all offer free budget templates that fully cover manual budgeting. You pay only for convenience: about $99/year for Tiller's automatic bank sync, or a one-time $9, $29 for a polished ready-made template.
Is Tiller worth it over a free template?
Only if automatic bank sync is what you're missing. Tiller ($99/year, 30-day trial) imports and categorizes your transactions automatically. A free template does the same budgeting math but you enter transactions yourself. If manual entry is why you've quit budgeting before, Tiller is worth it.
Spreadsheet or an app like YNAB?
A spreadsheet wins for $0 cost, full control, and no bank linking. An app wins for automation and a phone-first experience. YNAB is $14.99/month or $109/year with a 34-day trial; a manual spreadsheet is free. Choose the app if manual entry makes you quit; otherwise a spreadsheet does the job.
Which budgeting method should I use?
Start with 50/30/20 (50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings and debt, per NerdWallet) for simplicity. Move to zero-based budgeting for tight control, or plan-vs-actual to review targets against reality. All three work in any spreadsheet.
How many downloads does Vertex42 have?
Vertex42's Money Management Template page states 'Over 750,000 downloads' for the original version and 'Over 250,000 downloads' for version 2.1. Both are free under a personal-use license.
What does Tiller cost?
$99 per year after a 30-day free trial, per Tiller's own pricing page. Your card isn't charged until the trial ends, and Tiller states there are 'never any ads or hidden fees.' It connects your bank to Google Sheets or Excel.
What's the cheapest way to budget in a spreadsheet?
Free. Download a Vertex42 template, use the free Google Sheets or Excel budget template, or the CFPB Budget Worksheet. All cost $0 and require only manual transaction entry.
Is it safe to link my bank to Tiller or a budgeting app?
Reputable tools use read-only aggregation and let you revoke access, but if you'd rather not link accounts at all, a manual spreadsheet avoids the question. The CFPB publishes guidance on financial data sharing that's worth reading before you connect anything.
